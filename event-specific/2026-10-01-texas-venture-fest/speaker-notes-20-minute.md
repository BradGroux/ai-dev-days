# Speaker Notes: 20-Minute Version

Panel: “AI Developer Ecosystems,” Texas Venture Fest Houston 2026 — The AI Edition. Thursday October 1, 1:40 PM CT.

## 1. AI developer ecosystems

0:00–1:00. Open with where you just were: two days earlier you were at OpenAI DevDay in San Francisco. The tools changed again — but the interesting question for builders is not which model won the week. It is which ecosystem you build in, because the ecosystem decides what your work compounds into.

## 2. A model is a demo. An ecosystem is a business.

1:00–3:30. Four parts. The model is table stakes and commoditizing fast. The harness — the agent loop of plan, act, verify, remember — is where the work happens. The tools and integrations are the leverage: what the agent can actually touch. And trust — distribution, security, who vouches for the work — is what enterprises actually buy. A demo shows a model. A business needs all four.

## 3. Five ecosystems. One operator.

3:30–6:30. Walk the map. OpenAI: frontier models, Codex, the DevDay platform — the gravity well everything else orbits. OpenClaw: the local-first control plane; your machine runs your agents with durable context, bounded access, and a visible workflow. Buzz: open-source tooling you contribute to and teach. Muse: Meta’s personal agent — general-purpose help that works for one person. GitHub: where the developers already are, plus the Secure Open Source Fund backing the maintainers everything depends on. One operator — you — choosing deliberately.

## 4. DevDay, day after

6:30–8:30. [TBD — written Sep 30 after DevDay.] The 2–3 announcements that actually move this map, what you tested hands-on at Fort Mason, and the operator’s read: adopt, watch, or ignore. Keep it to what changed a decision, not a recap.

## 5. Codex: your agent, everywhere

8:30–10:30. Read the line: Dots made agents always-on. Spaces made them shared. Codex is the builder's side of the same shift — the agent leaves the chat box and runs where your work lives. Three ideas the room should keep: every surface (your machine, your phone, or the cloud), reusable team environments (build once, the whole team runs it), and the Agents API in public beta (the managed Codex harness, computer use included). This is the developer on-ramp to the Dots and Spaces world you just described.

## 6. GitHub is still where developers live

10:30–13:00. Your history here is the credential. Secure Open Source Fund cohort — funding that treats maintainers like infrastructure. The creator demo at GitHub HQ during Build week. Two days staffing the open source zone at Microsoft Build, talking to the people who ship. The point for the room: the largest developer distribution on earth, and it vouches for the work. Ecosystems are not just technology — they are trust networks.

## 7. Own the method. Rent the tool.

13:00–15:00. The decision rule. Own your process, your data, and your judgment — those are the assets. Rent the models, the tools, the platforms — those change every quarter. And verify everything that touches production. This is how you pick from the map on slide 3 without marrying a vendor.

## 8. Verifiable work, human in the loop

15:00–17:00. Where this goes. The next moat is not the model — it is the workflow you can audit. Agents that show their work earn enterprise trust; black boxes do not, no matter how good the demo looks. Human in the loop is not a brake on progress — it is the feature that lets agents into real businesses.

## 9. Ship one thing in public

17:00–20:00. Close with the assignment. Pick one ecosystem from the map. Build one useful thing with it this week. Publish the trail — that is how the opportunities on slide 6 happened for you. Start small, think big. Leave the Follow/Visit block up for photos.
