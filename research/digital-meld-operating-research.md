## Source Facts

### Operating Framework

The framework:

- applies six concerns to business work: Intent, Responsibility, Work, Control,
  Assurance, and Learning;
- keeps accountable human ownership explicit when AI participates;
- treats technology as part of the operating environment rather than the
  framework;
- requires material sources, context, decisions, state, evidence, handoffs,
  and lessons to survive beyond individual memory or temporary model context;
- uses Understand, Document, Validate, Approve, Use, and Improve to maintain
  standards; and
- identifies AI Dev Days as a related research and education initiative that
  may teach the framework but cannot redefine it.

### People and Real Work

Digital Meld's process article reports that official and working processes
often differ, especially around exceptions. It identifies source notes, SOPs,
PRDs, deterministic checks, human approval, and feedback loops as useful
operating artifacts. It recommends beginning with one painful workflow and one
reviewable artifact rather than a broad autonomous-agent roadmap.

The partner-journey article states that AI adoption depends on fitting
technology around a business, its people, workflows, data, security boundaries,
approval points, support ownership, and feedback loops. It recommends learning
one workflow from the people who perform it before selecting tools.

### Permissions and Authority

The permissions article treats agent access as a task-level operating decision.
It distinguishes read-only review, normal implementation, network-backed work,
and exceptional full local access. It also states that local execution
permissions do not replace separate controls for browsers, connectors, cloud
systems, production credentials, external messages, deployments, or other
mutations.

### Evidence, Exceptions, and Human Approval

The accounts-payable article distinguishes:

- AI used to interpret language and prepare context;
- deterministic checks used for facts that should produce repeatable answers;
- automation used to move, record, and reconcile approved work; and
- accountable people used for authority and ambiguous business decisions.

It also describes a public, fictional scenario that preserves original sources,
names the evidence behind each classification, routes exceptions to an owner,
and stops before financial approval or payment.

## Analysis

The public Digital Meld material is consistent with the framework, but it is
practice evidence rather than framework authority.

The most useful educational translation is not a Digital Meld-branded delivery
method. It is a set of tested teaching postures:

- investigate actual work with practitioners;
- begin with one bounded outcome;
- make sources and authority visible;
- distinguish AI interpretation from deterministic verification;
- design stop conditions and recovery before granting action;
- create a review artifact that a responsible person can evaluate;
- preserve the result and lesson outside the model conversation; and
- let evidence justify later expansion.

This posture makes AI literacy operational. Learners practice judgment,
evidence, permission boundaries, and continuation rather than only learning
prompt syntax or product navigation.

## Relevance to AI Dev Days

AI Dev Days should:

- treat learners and practitioners as sources of operating knowledge;
- begin an event or lab with a workflow, owner, outcome, and boundary;
- teach prompts as one artifact inside an operating context, not as the
  process;
- require source-aware and reviewable outputs;
- use deterministic checks when an answer should be repeatable;
- make permission modes, external-action gates, and stop conditions visible;
- use fictional or approved public examples for demonstrations;
- separate event evidence from approved reusable curriculum; and
- use post-event review to disposition lessons.

These implications are implemented in
[`docs/research-and-education-method.md`](../docs/research-and-education-method.md),
the event template, and the contribution SOP.

## What Should Be Reused

- The practitioner-first posture.
- One bounded workflow and one reviewable first artifact.
- Explicit sources, owners, authority, exceptions, and stop conditions.
- Separate roles for AI interpretation, deterministic verification,
  automation, and human decision.
- Public-safe fictional scenarios.
- Evidence and feedback loops that support later improvement.
- Tool choice after the work and boundary are understood.

## What Should Not Be Copied

- Digital Meld commercial offers, pricing, qualification, or delivery language.
- Private client records, client-identifying stories, or internal operating
  material.
- A vendor-specific architecture as a general program requirement.
- Codex permission-profile names as a universal security model.
- Financial, safety, legal, security, or other professional claims beyond the
  documented review.
- Anecdotal outcomes represented as measured learner or business results.

## Remaining Gaps

- The complete research-to-education method has not yet been measured through a
  new event from planning to post-event disposition.
- Historical events did not use a common learner-outcome collection method.
- Accessibility review depth varies across event packets and generated decks.
- Domain review is event-specific and should not be inferred from publication.
- Tool-neutral tracks beyond the current OpenClaw and Codex material need
  development and validation.

## Recommendation

Accept the bounded lessons above as evidence for the AI Dev Days version 1.0.0
program method.

Do not represent this note as a Digital Meld commercial standard, an
independent validation of Digital Meld claims, or an amendment to the
AI-Native Operating Framework.
