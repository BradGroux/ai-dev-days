## Summary

The DOL framework defines AI literacy as foundational competencies that help people use and evaluate AI technologies responsibly, with an emphasis on generative AI in modern workplaces.

The framework is voluntary guidance for workers, employers, training providers,
teachers and faculty, state and local agencies, and workforce or education
stakeholders. It is intended to support flexible program design across
industries, roles, educational settings, and workforce contexts.

## Foundational Content Areas

1. Understand AI Principles
2. Explore AI Uses Directly
3. Direct AI Effectively
4. Evaluate AI Outputs
5. Use AI Responsibly

## Effective Delivery Principles

1. Enable Experiential Learning
2. Embed Learning in Context
3. Build Complementary Human Skills
4. Address Prerequisites to AI Literacy
5. Create Pathways for Continued Learning
6. Prepare Enabling Roles
7. Design for Agility

## AI Dev Days Curriculum Implications

- Preserve the first working loop because experiential learning is the strongest delivery anchor.
- Keep exercises tied to attendee goals and Beaver Badges so AI use is embedded in context.
- Treat Markdown memory, agent roles, and task lists as the practical mechanics of directing AI effectively.
- Keep contracts, reviewer rubrics, and repair loops prominent because evaluation is a core AI literacy skill.
- Make publication safety, credential handling, and least-privilege roles visible throughout the workshop.
- Give facilitators, helpers, and reviewers explicit support materials because they are enabling roles.
- Keep modules modular and refreshable because AI literacy content must adapt as tools and workplace use cases change.
