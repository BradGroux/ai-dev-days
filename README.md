AI Dev Days is the public research and education companion to the AI-Native
Operating Framework. It helps people investigate, define, perform, verify, and
improve useful work with AI.

The [AI-Native Operating Framework](https://github.com/BradGroux/ai-native-operating-framework)
is the program's operating foundation. It keeps business purpose, human
accountability, controls, evidence, shared operating memory, and learning ahead
of any particular technology. AI Dev Days teaches and applies the framework; it
does not redefine it.

OpenClaw is the deepest existing tool-specific track in this repository.
Codex and other tools also appear in event material. Tools provide the
implementation context, not the program identity.

The point is simple: get people to one reviewable operating win quickly, then
show them how to extend and improve it.

The program follows a source-grounded research-to-education method. Raw research
has no authority on its own, event experience does not become reusable
curriculum automatically, and every material lesson receives an accountable
disposition.

## What this curriculum is trying to do

- Turn source-grounded research and practitioner knowledge into useful learning
- Connect AI activity to a clear purpose, owner, outcome, and boundary
- Get attendees from zero to one useful, reviewable operating loop quickly
- Teach operator workflow, not just prompts or terminal commands
- Keep mixed-skill rooms moving together
- Leave behind reusable material other organizers can run with
- Preserve evidence, handoffs, and lessons that survive the event
- Give every material event lesson a visible disposition
- Convert curiosity into accountable practice after the event

The curriculum is aligned to the U.S. Department of Labor's AI Literacy Framework:
understand AI principles, explore AI uses directly, direct AI effectively,
evaluate AI outputs, and use AI responsibly. See the
[AI Literacy Framework alignment](docs/ai-literacy-framework-alignment.md) for
the source citation and workshop mapping.

## Core stance

- **First success beats feature coverage.** If people don’t get a working loop early, the rest doesn’t matter.
- **Business purpose comes before technology.** The tool is part of the operating environment, not the framework.
- **One standard serves people and AI.** Clear operating documentation should work for every authorized participant.
- **Scenario-first beats blank slate.** Give people a concrete use case instead of a vague platform tour.
- **Evidence beats activity.** A completed exercise should leave a reviewable result, not only a demo.
- **Research remains traceable.** Sources, interpretation, uncertainty, and
  decisions stay distinguishable.
- **Events produce evidence, not automatic standards.** Reusable changes require
  review and approval.
- **Workshop time is expensive.** Anything likely to explode should be preflighted, optional, or moved to helper lanes.

## Success criteria

A good Dev Days session ends with most attendees having:

- a business or personal outcome stated clearly
- an accountable human owner and visible decision boundaries
- one useful people-and-AI workflow or scenario attempted
- one reviewable artifact, result, or evidence record
- durable context sufficient to continue the work
- one lesson or improvement captured
- a clear next step after the event

Tool-specific event packets add their own completion criteria. For an OpenClaw
track, those criteria may include installation, onboarding, gateway,
dashboard, and first-response verification.
